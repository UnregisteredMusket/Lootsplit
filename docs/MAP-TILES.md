# Maps, party travel and combat tiles

Selected map icons use a blue fill and ring. Selecting another record moves the highlight; **Close details** clears a record selection. DM combat-token selection is also highlighted when chosen on the map or in the **Combat token** list. The highlight is local to your view and changes no campaign data.

**Maps workspace** is the default route, filling `100dvh` without a native fullscreen permission request. Its floating title/navigation capsule, top search/menu, right scene/zoom groups and expandable horizontal hotbar follow the owner-specified Project Zomboid Map reference. Settings overlays the full-size canvas and contains the former management page: map library/categories/import/edit, parent/default/entrances/records, grid calibration, access/movement, saved/shared views, filters, labels/media and optional player display. Forms stay mounted when Settings is hidden, including the no-map empty state before a first import. Services, token inspector and encounters retain their canonical overlays and lazy loading. The six main destinations remain in the map navigation menu; X Close map (upper right) leaves map mode (see Map controls).

On portrait phones, Settings shares the title row, search spans the next row, scene navigation is horizontal, and camera controls sit above the bottom tool dock. The primary tools scroll horizontally while More map tools stays visible; the collapse chevron replaces the ambiguous duplicate pencil. All targets remain at least 44px, including 36px marker symbols inside 44px targets. Narrow landscape retains separate edge controls. Camera restoration and Fit map are unchanged: deliberately zoomed-out or panned views can show the background beyond the artwork.

Personal last-map and normalized per-map cameras use `map-view-state.ts`, keyed by account, campaign/database, room, role and sorted assigned characters. Guests add seat/session identity and use document memory only. Permission-filtered maps constrain requested and remembered IDs before rendering; revoked/deleted IDs fall back to an accessible active scene or existing location/party/first-map choices. Shared-camera nonces apply once; DM browsing never writes another viewer’s state. Initial views cover the canvas without stretching; Fit map explicitly fits the whole artwork. Image readiness prevents wrong-aspect/camera flashes. Resize/rotation preserves normalized world center and rendered map scale. Personal camera records retain their fit-width basis, so moving between desktop and phone dimensions does not shrink the artwork into a narrow band; the fit-relative percentage can change. Older camera records without a basis remain readable. Shared and saved starting-view command formats are unchanged. Native fullscreen changes do not alter workspace state.

Hotbar: Pan, Select/move, Ping, Measure/templates, marker placement, personal drawing/text, and grouped fog/walls/lights, existing-record placement, grid, encounters and fog undo/redo. Keyboard V/S/P/M/T, / search, H hide, Escape close/cancel; existing arrows/Alt-arrows/Enter/Ctrl-Enter remain. Drawings are explicitly personal device notes with undo/redo; shared annotations remain canonical map markers. These local notes never enter campaign projections or financial commands. Guests do not write them to persistent storage. Search uses accessible maps and the same canonical pin resolution, with type and scene context. Recenter is camera-only.

Browser address bars cannot be hidden reliably across embedded/mobile browsers; full page coverage does not depend on hiding them. Optional TV/player-display retains its independent browser fullscreen controls. Android 1.8.0 and later bundle the Maps workspace, connected scenes, encounter handoff, protected map media, animation controls, fog and drawing tools (`changelog.json`). A later website-only change reaches the installed app in its next signed release.

The six existing tabs and all existing map tools remain available. Old maps without a category or grid continue to load. City/town-linked old maps appear in City; other old maps appear in Regional.

Import or edit maps as Regional, City, Dungeon, Property, Wilderness, Battle, Ship or Other. Filter by type above the map selector. Dungeon and property floor plans use the same image import, reviewed anchors, markers, pings, records and map tools. A property/ship map can link to a canonical holding without requiring a new market location.

The DM's **Move party freely** control places the party at a normalized image point. Choose Roads, Wilderness with Forest/Grassland/Desert/Mountains/Hills/Swamp/Arctic/Tundra/Jungle/Savanna/Coastal/Underdark, River, Sea, Coast, Underground, Dungeon, Settlement or Other; add a description for a specific trail or environment. This clears the linked party location. It closes located shops/listings without changing legacy campaignwide records, inventories, physical ship boarding or individual character positions. Pending downtime must first be approved or cancelled. Selecting an existing party location ends free placement. Private maps redact coordinates and image from players while sharing the travel environment.

Enable **Combat grid settings** in the map editor. Set columns, rows, feet per tile (default **5 ft**), diagonal distance and the grid rectangle's percentage offsets/size. Align the grid to the image, including any border. Save to show the grid/token layer; **Leave tile mode** / **Enter tile mode** toggles it. The DM adds canonical character/ship tokens or custom tokens. Select a token, choose its tile by tapping the map or typing a one-based column/row, then save the move. The distance readout uses the configured diagonal rule. The DM decides movement allowance, terrain, collisions and other combat rulings; this feature does not enforce a ruleset or roll dice.

**Use encounter roster** loads encounters from the active DM campaign/device. Place the saved roster to copy only names and stable combatant identities into DM-only tokens, then share selected tokens or link them to canonical characters/ships. Existing tokens are retained and importing the same roster does not duplicate its combatants. HP, conditions, initiative, rounds and loot stay in the encounter tracker. Save tracker edits before refreshing its turn on the map. The encounter tracker links back to Maps. Players can inspect shared tokens/grid and use existing map navigation, filters and pings. Player movement is Automatic on new maps and off on maps configured before 2026-10-29 until the DM turns it on (see Player token movement below). Private encounter statistics remain in the tracker.

Ship tokens reference the existing ship holding. When a ship token moves, its already placed aboard-character tokens retain their relative tile offsets; the entire move fails if any would leave the grid. Tokens do not create boarding, cargo, money, remote property access or travel progress. Use the existing boarding reviews, voyage controls or recorded DM teleport for those actions.

Ship voyages retain manual duration. Optionally choose a gridded route map with reviewed anchors for both ports, and supply intermediate `column,row; column,row` tiles. Distance and the ship's configured miles per day determine proposed duration. The DM reviews departure and approves downtime through existing campaign controls. Route markers derive from elapsed/duration along the captured route, stop when blocked, and reach the destination when the existing voyage arrives. Route geometry/scale/duration freeze while underway; later grid edits cannot change travel progress. Existing crew, damage, payer/budget, expenses, cargo, berth, arrival and teleport checks still apply. Combat placement never advances the voyage clock.

Changes use authoritative local/shared commands, stale checks and existing retry receipts. Turn-based actions follow the transaction turn; they are separate from encounter initiative. Old saves, backups and complete session archives retain optional new map data. Legacy journal clients cannot overwrite the new authoritative metadata. Move/clear a free party position or resolve ongoing mapped voyages before deleting their map; grids cannot be removed or shrunk past tokens. The shared web/mobile source supports these controls; Android 1.8.0 and later include them.

## Online tabletop workspace

Remote play uses normal account-owned Live/Turn-based rooms and current player invitations. Every participant opens Maps on their own device. TV output is optional and default-off; enable it explicitly in Settings → Map session status to expose the existing player display controls. The six tabs, themes, locations, encounters, loot, ships and economy remain canonical.

New map drafts start private. Review the artwork and explicitly check **Share this map image with players** before making it available. The session status in Settings distinguishes the viewed scene from the scene activated for players. Searchable map cards supplement the original selectors. DM browsing/preparation does not activate a map or move player cameras. **Activate for players** directs the scene; **Share this view** sends one deliberate camera action. **Save starting view** stores an initial scene camera. Players can pan independently afterward. Scene browsing can be limited to the active map.

**Select / move** supports dragging, Shift group selection and arrows when the canvas has focus. Alt + arrows pans. Inspector provides exact one-based coordinates, linked records, token locks, delegated controllers, size/color/labels, sight and darkvision. Players move only their own or delegated unlocked tokens, freely outside combat and on their turn during it, unless the DM sets the map's player movement to Off. Pending/conflicting moves retain a preview and use existing turn/retry/CAS handling. The original DM coordinate forms remain available.

**Measure** adds temporary waypoints; persistent circle, cone, line and rectangle templates require separate player permission. Both use the scene's scale/display units. They apply no damage, target selection, movement allowance, time, wealth or inventory changes. The existing encounter tracker remains authoritative for initiative, HP, conditions and awards. Mapped ship voyages retain approved downtime; combat movement does not board characters or change ports.

**Exploration & lighting** provides reveal/conceal brush, rectangle and polygon, whole-scene controls and 20 saved undo/redo states. Optional shared-party or assigned-character vision uses configured walls, doors, windows, secret doors, lights, ambient light and character sight/darkvision. Movement blocking is a separate segment option and has an explicit DM override. These are practical 2D tools, not complete rules adjudication or persistent explored-memory vision. Physical miniatures still require manual updates. Keyboard users pan to aim, Enter places at the center and Ctrl + Enter finishes a polygon; Escape cancels.

Private source pixels and geometry are excluded from player envelopes and unauthorized media reads. The DM publishes audience/revision-scoped raster views; concealed RGBA is zeroed before encoding. Visibility changes invalidate older renders and clients fail closed while waiting. Player-view preview is DM-local. A shared map without fog/vision exposes its complete artwork. A client cannot erase secrecy by removing a CSS mask. The authorized DM still controls publication and can intentionally share artwork.

## Detailed media and reviewed interchange

PNG/JPEG/WebP and MP4/WebM originals retain up to 20 MB, 8192 pixels per edge and 64 megapixels. Bounded thumbnails remain in campaign records; protected immutable original/render media uses separate authenticated chunks. Animated PNG/WebP and video start paused and provide play/pause; changing to reduced motion pauses playback. Concealed player views use a still raster. Missing/unsupported media reports an error with the existing preview retained; Retry map artwork retries only the failed artwork read. The optional TV receives a separate bounded presentation raster, prepared from its authorized still image or baked party view, with no campaign providers or credentials. Account media reservations are bounded at 2 GB; existing files are retained when full.

UVTT 0.2/0.3 (`.uvtt`, `.dd2vtt`, `.df2vtt`) imports accept up to 30 MB of JSON with an embedded PNG/WebP, integer 1–200 cell dimensions, up to 1,000 segments and 200 lights. Imports prepare a private, concealed draft. Review the grid, 5-foot default scale, walls/doors and light ranges before saving. Outside geometry is clipped/omitted with visible warnings. Object outlines become transparent movement barriers; intensity and exporter-specific effects are approximate. Unsupported extra metadata is disclosed and ignored; scripts, remote URLs, external files, executable modules, arbitrary actors and wallets are never imported. Import a new map when replacing a scene that already has placed tokens.

Complete device backups include current and archived map media and verify checksums before replacement. Account backups upload artwork separately and store only references in the bounded backup record, then download/verify media before local restore. Account report downloads include only the historical seat's authorized media. Unreadable old archive bytes remain preserved. Old APK room/record envelopes omit unsupported tabletop fields after privacy projection; original server data and pending new commands remain retained. Detailed tabletop backups direct older clients to the updated website instead of silently losing media. Website source and installed APK versions remain separate; Android 1.8.0 and later include these map media controls.

Interchange references: [Arkenforge format notes](https://arkenforge.com/universal-vtt-files/) and [Encounter+ file types](https://docs.encounter.plus/reference/file-types/). No third-party map artwork or code is bundled.

## Connected exploration and services

Settings → Connected maps & places assigns a parent scene and optional explicit Regional/Local/Battle default for the map's canonical location. Existing category choices remain unchanged. Location icons open an unambiguous local scene; explicit entrance pins select an exact destination and optional entry camera. Back, breadcrumbs and the scene selector work inside the persistent Maps workspace. Cameras are remembered per scene and scoped participant/campaign context. Browsing, physical party movement and activation for players are distinct actions.

Place an existing record by selecting its kind and exact ID-backed picker, choosing a point and saving its visibility. An explicit placement overrides its inherited location anchor; removing the placement restores the inferred position. A pin is not a new business, wallet, stock list or location assignment. Open in map reuses existing shop/smith, NPC dialogue/barter/training, property/storage/projects, listing purchase, commodity exchange, postal and ship controls. Existing links and standalone screens remain. Players need both source-map visibility/fog access and the canonical record/service permission, including physical availability.

Start encounter here reviews a saved encounter, map, calibrated grid/theatre mode and whether to show players. Preparation is saved before encounter start. An interrupted start/activation offers Finish opening battle map and checks the same encounter before retrying. Submit pending turn-based commands before continuing. The tracker stays DM-only; the player combat HUD projects only lifecycle, round and a currently revealed token ID. Conclude & review loot uses the original award review. Return to exploration restores the saved map/camera, retaining tokens/fog and changing neither travel time nor wealth. No name-based character/HP matching occurs.

Connected-map metadata and combat presentation use tabletop protocol 2. Compact fog history and continued brush strokes use tabletop protocol 3 (see Fog storage below). Tabletop protocol 4 sends shared map images as references instead of inline rasters (see Map images on the wire below). Older clients receive their existing protocol shape, cannot silently erase the new fields, and must update before restoring a backup containing unsupported metadata (protocol 2 backups expand losslessly and are served directly). No database migration is needed.

## Journey planner (October 23, 2026)

Owner-approved mockups (`route-toolbar.html`, `journey-review.html`, `journey-progress.html`) and decisions, 2026-10-23 (design notes §9 answered "as shown"). Route maths: `src/lib/quire/journey.ts` (pure, shared by the planner, the voyage form, the downtime quote and the commands). UI: `src/components/map-route-planner.tsx`.

- **Plan route (phase A).** A hotbar tool for everyone who can open the map. Taps add waypoints: within 3.5% of the image height of an anchored place they snap to it (dashed ring, `snapRoutePoint`), and on a gridded map to the tile centre. A tap near a leg inserts a waypoint there; tapping a waypoint removes it; dragging moves it; a pin tapped while planning adds its place. Each handle is a 44px target. Type instead adds anchored places or `x%, y%` points; the keyboard path is Enter (add at the crosshair) and Backspace (remove the last). Every change is announced ("Waypoint 4 added, leg 3 14.4 miles"). The leg list is the text alternative: distance, days at the pace (two decimals) and each leg's terrain (environment and biome, the same enum and rules as `partyMapPositionSchema`; chosen, never read from the artwork). Drawing changes nothing in the campaign. Desktop: a panel on the left; 650px and narrower: a bottom sheet above the dock; short landscape: a left panel.
- **Finding the planner.** Plan journey (J) in the map tools (the right rail on desktop and landscape phones, the bottom bar on phones; one entry since the map controls redesign removed the floating pill), Plan a journey in the Here sheet at the party's location, and Travel at the top of the Downtime screen (`journeyPlanHref`: `/maps?map=<party map>&plan=party`, the map the party is placed on or one anchoring its location, scaled maps first). On phones the camera zooms out once (`frameRoute`, never zooming in) so the map or the drawn route shows in the band above the sheet when the planner opens or switches to calibration; wider screens keep their camera. The journey review is full height on phones. Players with no shared map get the text-only journey card on Maps.
- **Distances.** Whole tenths of a mile per leg (`legDeciMiles`). A grid always wins: legs are measured tile to tile with the map's diagonal rule (the alternating count carries across waypoints, exactly as voyages' `routeLegs`), `feetPerTile ÷ 5,280` miles a tile; a point outside the grid is refused. Otherwise the scale bar: straight lines in the image's true proportions. Duration is whole minutes: `ceil(deci × 144 × 8 ÷ (milesPerDay × (8 + forced-march hours)))`, so 61.1 mi at 24 mi/day = 3,666 min (three downtime days), and at grid scale it equals the voyage formula `ceil(feet ÷ (mpd × 5280) × 1440)` when tiles are whole tenths of a mile.
- **Scale bar.** `CampaignMap.scale = { a, b, distance, unit: "mi"|"km"|"ft", aspect }`, optional. `a`/`b` are normalized points and `aspect` is the image's width ÷ height when calibrated, so `distance(p,q) = hypot((q.x−p.x)·aspect, q.y−p.y) ÷ hypot((b.x−a.x)·aspect, b.y−a.y) × distance`. DM only, saved with the existing `map-save` (CAS on `before`); Measure uses it on ungridded maps. Journeys under way keep the scale they were approved with (phase B freezes it). **Tabletop protocol 6** (`MAP_SCALE_PROTOCOL`): current clients declare 6 and the account client sends `x-lootsplit-tabletop: 6`; protocols 0–5 receive maps (and report snapshots) without `scale`; their map saves and whole-journal writes keep it; a pending newer map save with a scale tells an older app to finish it on the updated website; an account backup with a scale asks protocol 0–5 clients to restore on the updated website. No migration.
- **Party journeys (phase B).** `world.journeys[]` (history kept; at most one open: proposed, planned, active or arrived) and `world.travel` (paces 18/24/30 mi/day DM-editable, forced march off with up to 8 extra hours when allowed, catalogue prices for "Day's trail rations" 5 cp and "Nosebag of oats" 8 cp). Commands, all through `commands.ts` (offline runs the same engine): `travel-settings` (DM, CAS), `party-journey` (DM; a player only proposes, edits or withdraws their own request, time only), `downtime-plan` + `journeyId` (freezes the route: frame and fingerprint, leg deci-miles, duration, travellers, unit prices; status `planned`), `downtime-apply` (unchanged command; charges the optional lines once and moves the party), `party-location` + `journeyId` (arrival). Engine: `src/lib/quire/journey-engine.ts`; UI: `src/components/map-journeys.tsx`.
- **Quote and approval.** `quote.journey` = journey ID and hash, minutes from/to, arrival, the party position at the end of the window, optional lines (`rations`: travellers × days × unit; `mounts`: chosen held animals × days × unit; whole copper, paid after every other quote line) and notices (located shops left behind, schedule closures during travel and at arrival, characters who stay behind). `applyDowntime` recomputes and compares the whole quote, so a changed balance, journey, map or scale refuses a stale preview. A payer short of coins is marked `short` and the approval refuses before anything moves ("Nothing was charged"); no arrears. Ledger lines are `<downtimeId>:journey:<kind>`. Departure clears the party's location exactly like Move party freely (located shops close); `world.partyPosition` follows the frozen route with the current leg's terrain. Apply fewer days to stop mid-route; Prepare remaining days plans the rest from the same frozen route. Only approved downtime advances a journey; Session Time never does (the voyage rule).
- **Arrival and stopping.** Arrival sets `arrived`; the DM confirms Move party here (`party-location{journeyId}` to the destination: no time, no money, journey `completed`) or chooses Not yet (the party stays on the road at the route's end). Stop the journey here (`active|arrived → stopped`) keeps the position. While a journey is active or arrived, plain party-location and Move party freely are refused, and its map can't be removed (planned too).
- **Review Inbox.** Player requests and DM drafts appear in Review Inbox (Bank). Deny takes an optional reason with Show the reason to the player. Approve & depart · N days is one atomic `applyCommandBatch` of `downtime-plan{journeyId, expectedQuoteHash}` and `downtime-apply` (shared: one direct room submission; offline: one IndexedDB transaction); if the quote differs from the one the DM was shown it stops and asks to open the full review. Open full review links to `/maps?map=…&plan=journey:<id>`. Journey plans never advance a trade season (its rolls would change the quote); use the downtime screen for seasonal downtime.
- **Privacy (`projectRecord`, which `view()` uses for players).** Players receive their own requests and the party's reviewed journeys: status, days, progress, each leg's terrain, pace, their own message and a shared denial reason. Never: other players' requests or messages, unshared reasons, payers or mounts they don't control, DM prices (`travel` keeps only paces and forced march) or destinations they don't know. Private map: no `mapId`, waypoints, frame or distances (a text-only card). Shared map with concealed fog or vision: waypoints are cut on the server and only visible stretches are sent as `runs` (24 samples a leg). Pending downtime previews are never projected to players.
- **Tabletop protocol 7** (`JOURNEY_PROTOCOL`): protocols 0–6 receive the world without `journeys` and `travel`; their whole-journal writes keep them; pending journey commands (or `journeyId` on downtime-plan / party-location) ask older apps to finish on the updated website; account backups with journeys ask protocol 0–6 clients to restore on the website.
- **Split the party (2026-10-25, tabletop protocol 10; docs/WORLD-FEATURES.md).** A journey moves one group (`journey.groupId`, absent = group A): at most one open journey per group (`validateJourneys`), travellers are that group's members, "left behind" shops are at that group's place, and "stays behind" names characters "with <group>". Departure moves the group's own `position` (group A: `partyPosition`, as before); arrival is `party-location{groupId, journeyId}` ("Move <group> here"). Players plan only for their own character's group; the DM picks the group in the planner. One campaign clock: `downtime-plan` may add `journeyIds` (other groups' journeys the DM ticks under "Other groups travelling" in the review), quoted in `quote.journeys` and applied in order (their ledger lines and events are keyed `…journey:<journeyId>…`); unticked journeys stay put. A hidden group's journeys are projected only to its own players and the DM; protocol 9 apps never receive other groups' journeys or `quote.journeys`. The map draws one Party marker per group with its letter and colour (`mapGroupPins`), the register offers "Move <group> here" per group, and the Here sheet has a group switch ("Whose place").
- **Ship voyages.** Gridded maps only (owner decision; an ungridded `mapPath` is not built). Ship voyage mode lists docked ships the viewer may command at a place anchored on the map and anchored destinations; the route runs from the origin anchor's tile to the destination's, taps add intermediate tiles, and the existing typed `column,row; …` field (1-based) stays in sync both ways. Use in voyage request hands `mapRoute`, destination and the typed tiles to that ship's existing voyage form (an in-memory draft, `voyage-route-draft.ts`) and opens it; payer, budget, crew, cargo, berth and duration checks are unchanged. The form's Draw this route on the map opens `/maps?map=<id>&plan=ship:<propertyId>`.

## Fog storage and limits

Limits per map: 500 fog shapes, 200 tokens, 1,000 walls, 200 lights, 200 area templates; a campaign holds up to 100 maps. Fog undo and redo each keep 20 steps.

- **Strokes.** Brush points are rounded to 4 decimal places of the map size (under 1 px on an 8192 px map) and simplified with Ramer–Douglas–Peucker at a tolerance of 5% of the brush radius (0.0001–0.0008 of the map). A stroke longer than 200 saved points continues in further brush shapes (up to 20) that share their junction point, so the painted coverage is the same as one unbroken stroke; the whole stroke is one `map-fog` command (`shape` plus `continued`) and one undo step. A stroke at its maximum length shows a notice instead of silently stopping. Logic: `src/lib/quire/fog-stroke.ts`.
- **History.** `fogUndo`/`fogRedo` entries are compact diffs (`{kind:"fog-diff", keep, add, enabled, base}`): the restored state is the current fog's first `keep` shapes plus `add`. Each entry is relative to the state above it on its stack; undo and redo restore exactly what the earlier full snapshots restored (`fog-storage.test.ts` checks this against the old engine). Rooms saved earlier keep their full-snapshot entries, which stay readable and are converted exactly on the next fog write; history that cannot be read safely is kept as it is, and the current fog is never dropped. Whole-tabletop saves (`map-tabletop`, `map-save`) read supplied history relative to the fog in their `before` and re-encode it. Logic: `src/lib/quire/fog-history.ts`.
- **Older apps.** Tabletop protocol 3 marks clients that read diffs. Protocol 2 clients (Android 1.10.0 and earlier) receive full snapshots (`tableForProtocol`), and their whole-tabletop saves still pass the compare-and-set check against the expanded form. Pending protocol-3 fog commands are not handed to them.
- **Size.** Before this change, 100 freehand strokes stored about 17 MB of fog and history (20 full copies), far above the 1.8 MB room guard, which then refused every write to the room. They now store about 75 KB. The DM sees "Fog storage: N of 500 shapes · K KB including undo history" in Exploration & lighting, with a warning from 450 shapes or 400 KB.
- **Projection** is unchanged: players receive no fog geometry or history.
- **Consolidate fog** (owner-approved 2026-10-22). Exploration & lighting → **Consolidate fog…** computes a proposal on the DM's device and asks for confirmation with the shape count before and after and the resulting storage size. The rewrite is exact, never approximate: (1) a shape is removed only when every part of it is proven, by recursive cells with conservative inside/outside tests, to be painted over by a later shape or already in the shape's own state; a shape whose proof does not finish within its fixed budget is kept; a leading shape covering the whole map becomes the base state. (2) Same-kind rectangles (and earlier merged rectangular outlines) that can move together in the paint order (past same-kind shapes, or opposite shapes whose bounds they do not touch) merge into one outline per connected area using only their existing coordinates; areas with holes or more than 200 corners become exact rectangles. Brush strokes, ellipses and freehand polygons that still show are kept unchanged because polygons cannot reproduce their curves. Coverage is tested identical by rasterising before and after at several resolutions (pixel centres and exact edge points) under both the server rule (`manuallyVisible`, even–odd) and the renderer rule (non-zero), including random mixed fog and 500-shape fog. It is the `map-fog` action `consolidate`: the client sends `before` and its `result`; the server recomputes deterministically and refuses a mismatch, an unchanged fog, or a non-DM seat. The merge is one undo step (a compact diff); earlier undo entries are kept byte-for-byte (the oldest is dropped once 20 are stored, as for any fog change) and redo is cleared. Pending consolidations are not handed to protocol 2 apps. Logic: `src/lib/quire/fog-consolidate.ts`; tests: `fog-consolidate.test.ts`.

## Map images on the wire (tabletop protocol 4)

Shared map images (`table.journal.world.maps[].image`, inline `data:image/...` rasters of 8,000 or more base64 characters that no portrait also uses) are stored once per room in `campaign_room_images` (migration 0012), keyed by `/art/room-image-<sha256>.webp`. Until 2026-10-22 every room read re-inlined them and every changed poll sent all of them to every seat: a token move in a room with 10 shared maps of about 300 KB re-sent about 3.3 MB to each seat.

- **Protocol.** Protocol 4 is the protocol 3 table shape (compact fog history) plus image references. It is a separate number because protocol 3 clients, such as websites loaded before the change and APKs that bundle protocol 3, read inline images only. The website client sends 4. Protocols 0–3 keep receiving full inline envelopes, unchanged. The account client still declares 3 (account records and backups stay hydrated).
- **Server.** `roomState`/`pollRoom` at protocol 4 read the room with `readRoom(code, { mapImageReferences: true })`. The current maps' references are not hydrated or read from D1. Drafts, archived snapshots and resource packs are hydrated as before. Write responses (`submitCommands`, pace, turn, manage) work on the fully hydrated room. `tabletopResponse` then swaps each current map image back to the reference it was read from (`withImageReferences`), using a non-enumerable map attached by `hydrateRoomImages`. Only images restored from the room's own rows are swapped, and an image written in the same request stays inline once. Exports, backups, recovery, account records, the multiplayer probe and every write keep the fully hydrated room. The stored-body guard (`roomStorageBody`, #131) is the raw row as before; it is never a hydrated or referenced body.
- **Fetch.** `GET /api/room-image/CODE/HASH` (`src/routes/api.room-image.$code.$hash.ts`, `room-image-route.server.ts`) takes the seat token in `x-lootsplit-seat`, never in the URL. It runs `guardMemberSeat` (owner account for the DM, linked-account restrictions) and then `authorizedRoomImage`. That function builds the seat's own projection (the same `seatTable` that `view` sends) and serves the image only if that projection has a current map with exactly this reference. It then reads the raster only from this room's rows. Private and hidden maps, maps replaced by protected-media posters, other campaigns and non-seats get 403/404 with `no-store`. The body is the exact stored data-URL text (`text/plain`), so the restored table is byte-identical to the server's copy. The strong ETag is the hash. `Cache-Control: private, no-cache` makes each reuse revalidate (a bodiless 304), so a released or hidden-map seat cannot reuse a browser-cached copy. The Android app reaches it through the same rewrite and CORS middleware as server functions (`isWorkerApiPath`).
- **Client.** `accept()` in `cloud-client.ts` restores references (`room-image-cache.ts`) before the table is read. Restoration uses document memory first, then an IndexedDB cache (`lootsplit.room-images.v1`, scoped to the campaign key and room code; members only), then the GET. Each download's SHA-256 is checked against its reference. Guests (ephemeral campaigns) keep images in memory only. Device storage, previews, backups and commands therefore always hold inline images, exactly as before. If any image cannot be loaded, the client reads the room once more as protocol 3 (full inline). `MapArtwork` shows "Loading map artwork…" while downloads are in progress. The server refuses a submitted command that still contains a room image reference instead of storing it as artwork.
- **Offline/device play** never sees references.
- **Measured** (`room-image-references.test.ts`; 10 shared maps plus 1 private, about 300 KB each, after a token move): DM response 3,302,875 → 3,568 bytes; player 3,002,756 → 3,386 bytes. Each image then downloads once per device (or document, for guests).
- **No migration**, and no new Cloudflare service: images stay in D1.

## Protected player view: render wait

With fog or vision on, every visibility change (each token move with vision, each fog change) bumps the map's tabletop revision and invalidates the previous player frames; players fail closed until the DM's device renders and publishes the current frame for each audience (`party`, or one per character with individual vision). `src/lib/quire/map-render-status.ts` holds the pure parts:

- **Debounce.** The publisher (`map-visibility-publisher.tsx`) waits 600 ms after the last change before rendering, but never more than 2 s after the first unrendered change, so rapid moves render once and continuous dragging still updates. A change during a render supersedes it. A failure stays reported until a render succeeds; the next change retries automatically, and **Retry player view** restarts at once.
- **Players** see "Waiting for the DM's device to update the map…" instead of a silently concealed map. The state is derived from projected data only: the placeholder image and the absence of a current-revision frame (`awaitingPlayerView`). No fog geometry is sent.
- **The DM** sees a badge on the map workspace when a render has been pending for 1.5 s (players are waiting) or has failed (with Retry player view), in shared rooms.

## Alternating diagonal (5/10/5)

Grids have a fourth diagonal rule, `alternating`: straight steps cost one tile, and every second diagonal step costs two (5, 15, 20, 30 ft for 1–4 diagonals on 5-ft tiles). The alternation carries across waypoints, so measuring through waypoints, voyage route distances and route progress (`routeLegs`) count it continuously. Token move readouts (tile form and drag preview), measuring and voyage route planning use the grid rule; vision, light ranges and area templates keep straight-line geometry. The schema enum is additive; old grids keep their rule and missing values still default to `equal`. Tabletop protocol 2 and older apps (Android 1.10.0 and earlier) see an alternating grid as `equal` (`legacyDiagonals`); their unchanged saves keep `alternating`, their token moves and combat starts are accepted against the grid fingerprint they can see, and pending commands containing the new rule are not handed to them. Logic: `tabletop-geometry.ts` (`diagonalTiles`, `sceneRouteDistance`) and `map-tiles.ts`; tests: `map-diagonal.test.ts`.

## Token footprints and snapping (map tokens PR 1, 2026-10-29)

- **Drawing.** Characters, creatures and custom tokens are round discs (portrait, else the initial; lower case for Small and Tiny) scaled to their footprint in map space, with the name chip below (`style.labels` still hides it) and a footprint square for sized or selected tokens (`src/components/map-token-layer.tsx`). Discs keep at least 28 px (16 px for Tiny) and a 44 px target. Ships keep their record icon. The DM's own tokens marked DM only are dashed; a player's own tokens have the accent ring; selection is a blue ring (`--color-select`).
- **Sizes.** `sizeClass` (tiny, small, medium, large, huge, gargantuan) and the DM's `footprint` (custom squares: 0.25 or 1–8) on the token; Tiny is a quarter square placed in a quadrant (offsets ±0.25), Small/Medium 1×1, Large 2×2, Huge 3×3, Gargantuan 4×4. Inspector → Size, Custom squares and Anchor (read-only).
- **Anchor.** A token wider than one square must have `anchor: "corner"`: `column`/`row` is its top-left square. Tokens without it keep the centre meaning, so nothing already placed moves; one square looks the same either way. The whole footprint must lie inside the grid (`tokenInsideGrid`, also checked on every save).
- **Snapping.** `snapTokenPosition(grid, token, point, grab)` (`tabletop-geometry.ts`) is the one rule: with `tabletop.snap` on the square under the pointer receives the footprint square that was grabbed, clamped inside the grid; Tiny takes the quadrant under the pointer. With snap off `freeTokenPosition` keeps the exact point. The server refuses positions that aren't snapped (`tokenSnapped`) or leave the grid; it never moves a token itself. Visibility and walls use the footprint centre.
- **While dragging** the token turns into a dashed ghost labelled "<name> · <size> (moving)", the target footprint is green with a dashed line, and "Drop: col 19–20, row 9–10 · 10 ft" names the squares and distance.
- **Encounters.** Combatants may carry `size` (from the Open5e SRD `size`); "Place encounter roster" places each creature at its footprint on the first free area.
- **Protocol 13.** Protocols 0–12 receive sized tokens in centre form (`centreFormToken`: centre square plus half a square for even sizes, no size fields), also in archived reports and account records; backups with sized tokens ask them to restore on the updated website. Their token moves, token saves and map saves are converted back to the stored form against the current table (`upgradeFootprintCommands`, `submitCommands`), so their saves keep the sizes. With snapping on an older app cannot express a snapped square for an even footprint, so its move of a Large or Gargantuan token is refused with "Update Lootsplitter to move <name> on this map." Pending drafts with size fields ask older apps to finish on the updated website.
- Tests: `src/lib/quire/token-footprints.test.ts`. Audit: `map-tokens-footprint` (`scripts/map-tokens-footprint-browser-audit.mjs`, fixture `scripts/map-tokens-fixture.mjs`).

## Grid mode (map tokens PR 1b, 2026-10-29)

- **Grid mode** is the shared `tabletop.snap` setting and draws the grid lines (the per-viewer Grid display switch still hides them locally; an encounter's grid presentation always shows them). DMs switch it in Layers › Grid mode or Inspector › Grid mode ("On · snap to squares" / "Off · move freely"); Scene permissions & vision keeps its "Snap movement to grid cells" box. Turning it off sends only `map-tabletop` and never moves a token.
- **Turning it on** with tokens between squares opens "Turn on grid mode" (`map-grid-mode.tsx`, `role="dialog"`, options as `role="radio"` with arrow keys, Esc cancels): (a) **Snap tokens to the nearest squares** (default): one DM command `map-grid-snap` `{ mapId, grid, before: settings, snap: true, moves[] }` checked against the settings and every token's position; each move must be snapped and inside the grid (`nearestSnapped`, at most half a square). Undo sends the inverse `map-grid-snap` (`snap: false`, moves reversed). (b) **Align the grid under the tokens**: one `map-save` with the grid shifted by the circular mean of the tokens' sub-square offsets and every token re-expressed at the same image point (`alignedGrid`; a column or row is dropped only when the shifted grid would leave the image); Undo saves the previous grid and tokens. "Just turn it on" keeps today's behaviour.
- **Automatic grid.** On a map without a grid, the Combat token tool offers "Add tokens with an automatic grid": a `map-save` with `automaticGrid` (5 ft squares from a feet scale bar, else about 40 image pixels, covering the image), grid mode off and `tabletop.gridHidden: true`. "Hide grid lines from everyone" in Scene permissions & vision shows or hides it.

## Player token movement (map tokens PR 2, 2026-10-29)

- **Rule (owner).** Movement is free outside combat; while an encounter is active on a map (`world.combat` active on it and the tracker row `active`), a player moves only the token whose combatant has the tracker's turn (`tokenTakesTurn`: its `combatantId`, or a character token whose purse is the active id); free again when combat ends. The DM always moves anything.
- **Per map** `tabletop.movement`: `auto` (Automatic), `off`, `approval` (Ask the DM, PR 8). Absent: Automatic when the map has no tabletop settings (new maps), else from `playerMovement`, so maps configured before stay off; Settings › Players & display shows "New: let players move their own tokens" with **Turn on Automatic** there (`MovementNotice`). Writes that leave `movement` out keep it, a map's first settings store Automatic, and `playerMovement` follows `movement` for older clients (`keepMovement`). Inspector › Scene permissions & vision: **Player movement** (Automatic / Off) and **Characters who can't move on this map** (`lockedPurseIds`, kept when the token is removed and placed again; players see only their own). The token Lock stays.
- **Server.** `canMoveMapToken` uses the mode and locks; `map-token-move` and the new `map-token-claim` check the turn with `execution.mapCombat`, which `submitCommands` reads once per attempt from `dm_encounters` (status and `activeId`) when a player's token command touches the combat map. Unreadable means refused: "The turn order is unavailable — ask the DM." A refusal names the acting token only when the player can see it ("Not your turn — Ogre is acting."). A device preview leaves the check to the room.
- **Claim.** `map-token-claim` `{ mapId, grid, purseId, tokenId, at }` places the seat's own character token: the character is theirs and visible, movement Automatic, not locked, no token of that character on the map yet, inside the grid, snapped, on a square the player can see, and on their turn during combat. "Place my token" in the player's movement chips, then a tap (or Enter at the crosshair).
- **Room turns.** In turn-based rooms a player's own `map-token-move`/`map-token-claim` are sent directly and accepted outside their room turn; initiative still applies.
- **Player view** (mockup player-token-move frames 1-3): chips "Free movement: no combat", "Fog: you can only move into what you can see", "Combat: turn-based movement" with "Speed N ft (from your sheet, shown as a guide)" and green reach squares on your turn, "Waiting: Mira is next after Bram" otherwise; your token is "Mira (you)" ("You" in combat), with a lock while waiting and a "Not your turn." card on a press; the drag card "20 ft · col 17, row 11 · Release to move". During a combat the DM shows on the map, the top-centre turn strip (Round N, Your turn: Mira / The Ogre is acting, the revealed order) replaces the status pill for players; the title shows "Combat".
- **Protocol 15.** `movement` and `lockedPurseIds` are stripped for protocols 0–14 (maps, reports, account records; backups with them ask them to restore on the website); their scene and map saves keep them, and their "Allow assigned-player token movement" change sets Automatic or Off. Pending claims or drafts with the fields ask them to finish on the updated website. The server enforces the rules for every protocol.
- Tests: `player-movement.test.ts`, `player-movement-room.test.ts`. Audit: `map-player-move`.
