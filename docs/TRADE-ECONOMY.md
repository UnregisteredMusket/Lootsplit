# Campaign trade economies

Open **Market → Trade Exchanges**. Each campaign owns its definitions, regional exchanges, stock, treasuries, seasonal rolls and history. Loading an older save does not enable trading or change its money. The existing shop and property location hierarchy controls where players may trade; a city exchange also serves its child areas. A cleared party location does not grant access to a located exchange.

The **Global market** overview on Market links each quote to its exact exchange and good. Trade Exchanges opens with a compact **Market board**: search goods, exchange names or locations; filter by category or exchange; show only open/active goods; and sort by name, asking price, stock or (DM) price change. Select a row for current ask/bid prices, stock, capacity, supply/demand and the existing buying/selling form. Stock/wanted bars use finite exchange capacity; supply and demand are indices with 100 neutral. Filters and row selection retain visited trade drafts. **Exchange directory** retains every available exchange's location, notes and DM treasury, including exchanges with no goods.

DM charts show recorded baselines and approved settlement prices, with Recent 12 / All recorded controls and an accessible data table. Current quote changes compare against the readable baseline before the current season, using the same exchange, commodity and physical unit. Missing/legacy baselines and changed units have no comparison; unrecorded gaps remain empty. Later DM edits change the current quote rather than rewriting plotted settlements. Player access remains current available quotes and their own receipts; private seasonal history and treasury balances remain private. All economy editors and manual controls follow the board under **DM economy controls**, and a pending-season link leads directly to its review. The shop directory, stock tools, price history, property stores, rules, finance and approval behavior remain available.

The DM enables the economy and creates commodities with a stable key, physical unit, category, base price and weight. Names do not identify interchangeable goods. A reviewed property-material link must use that material's existing unit. Production using that material can then generate commodity inventory; purchased bundles remain available to existing construction recipes and reservations. Use **Link existing inventory & shop activity** to review older lots explicitly. Traded unit/material identity stays fixed; create another commodity for a different unit.

Create regional exchanges with finite stock, seasonal target stock, capacity, supply/demand indices and bid/ask prices. Each exchange uses a separate canonical non-party treasury. Opening funds default to zero; the DM may explicitly fund it with an audited reason. No season automatically replenishes treasury money. Purchases and sales transfer actual copper between the character/party account and that treasury. Shop activity links preserve existing shop prices and quantities while recording next-season feedback.

The DM sees the actual exchange treasury balance. Players see that its balance is private; an omitted private account is never presented as an empty treasury. The authoritative transaction still checks whether the exchange can afford a purchase from the player.

## Classic and Automatic modes

Classic Mode keeps the DM's exact prices, stock and indices when the season advances. Automatic Mode prepares new supply/demand and stock with recorded d100 rolls, existing realm conditions, seasonal category pressure and that season's net purchases/sales. Buying increases next-season demand; selling has the opposite effect. Bid/ask prices remain fixed during a season until an explicit DM change. Existing realm price pressure is applied once, with editable minimum/maximum bounds. Each offer can lock prices, stock or indices independently.

Volatility, transaction influence, hardship chance/types, price bounds and buyback share are DM settings. Winter adds 10 hardship chance points; war and plague each add 5 per level, capped at 100. Default hardship types are shortage and roads; war/plague change automatically only when the DM enables them. Existing conditions remain until adjusted. These defaults are fictional game settings, not official D&D rules or prices.

The shared room server creates and stores rolls for the next season. Local DM play uses local cryptographic randomness. Recalculating, cancelling, reloading or retrying a preview retains that upcoming season's draws. Clients cannot supply dice results. Every approval checks the current authoritative state and records the proposal, applied results, reason and source. Receipts and original rolls remain in backups and session archives.

## End-of-session settlement

Open **Downtime**, choose the campaign days and keep **Settle one new market season with this downtime** selected. Review finances, property work, manager transactions and the seasonal proposal together. Approve downtime now, approve it and end the recorded session, or retain the existing approve-and-start-next-session workflow. Approval advances exactly one market season, regardless of downtime days. Changed funds, goods, staff, exchange settings or prices require recalculation. Pending/shared turn actions still follow the room's existing submission rules.

**Session Time** advances the campaign clock and approved within-session actions without settling a market season. **Manual season control & DM overrides** separately previews and approves any calendar season without advancing campaign days. Cancel pending downtime before manual settlement. The DM can override the proposed exchange values and realm conditions using the displayed JSON, retain individual offer locks, or change definitions/settings and prepare a fresh preview. Identity and treasury changes use the exchange workshop. Closing a room remains separate from ending a recorded session.

## Property stores, managers and letters

Purchases can enter accessible local property storage directly, subject to active storage and weight capacity. Sales use the character's own identified commodity lots. Stored goods require the property's location and access permission; reserved/transit goods cannot be sold. A player must follow withdrawal approval before selling from a restricted store. Physical custody, item ownership and original construction material references remain separate.

Under a property's **Letters & manager orders**, choose **Trade stored commodities at a local exchange**. Select the exchange, commodity, buy/sell direction, quantity and maximum buy/minimum sell price per unit. Use existing standing-order periods or send an instruction from an authorized post office. The manager needs the supplies duty, active employment, satisfied staff/wage requirements and an adequate budget. Orders use the property owner's actual purse and property location. The exchange must serve that location. Purchases enter storage; sales use the owner's unreserved stored lots, with finite treasury and capacity checks. Blocked orders report a reason and do not partly spend or sell. Postage is still paid once under existing postal terms; letters grant no remote carried-item access.

Property definitions continue to use the strict reviewed importer and sample in [PROPERTY-OPERATIONS.md](PROPERTY-OPERATIONS.md). Existing material keys/units connect those imported recipes to commodities; imports do not grant inventory, activate the economy, mint funds or fabricate seasonal history. Commodity/exchange configuration is reviewed in the workshop. Preserve full campaign backups when moving campaigns between devices.

### Supplier deliveries

Reviewed property suppliers use the shop's current selling rate, any enabled black-market premium, and the paying character's existing Charisma discount. Party accounts receive no Charisma discount. The manager's order and employment budgets include both the goods and the agreed delivery fee. A changed price, inadequate budget, missing goods, closed vendor or conflicting material identity blocks the order before it spends money or creates goods.

The existing purchased inventory lot keeps its reviewed material key and commodity identity while in transit and after arrival. An explicit shop-stock commodity/exchange link records the purchase once, for the goods' price only; delivery fees and postage remain in their existing finance records. A unique reviewed material-to-commodity binding can identify the goods without inventing exchange activity. Display names alone never establish a link. Disabled economies retain reviewed goods identity without adding activity.

Arrival and approved delivery retries update custody of the same goods. They do not purchase another lot or add demand again. Supplier and manager purchases performed during approved downtime are included in that downtime's seasonal proposal before approval, so the reviewed finances and market outcome describe the same activity.

## Correcting a recorded trade

The DM can open **Trade receipts**, choose **Review reversal**, enter a reason, and review the original transaction before choosing **Reverse trade**. New exchange purchases and sales retain an exact inventory snapshot and explicit references to both money movements. A reversal returns the original copper amounts, adjusts the original exchange stock, removes the unchanged purchased lot or restores the original sold lot, and records opposite activity in the current market season. It never recalculates the original price. Zero-copper trades can be corrected from their trade receipt as well.

Approval rechecks the exact goods, original accounts, available funds, stock/capacity, property ownership and any required storage capacity. Goods that have moved, changed, been reserved or been used cannot be safely reversed automatically. A manager order that sold several lots is corrected as one atomic operation; failure on any component leaves the whole correction unapplied. Its wages, postage, campaign days, spending budget and future schedule remain separate management decisions. Historical receipts, approved seasons and archive snapshots stay intact. Repeating the same correction does not create another refund, lot or activity record.

Supported ordinary shop purchases also return the exact purchased goods and finite shop stock, refund the buyer and correct any linked seasonal feedback. The existing ledger Void action uses the same authoritative command in local and shared play. Older receipts without a complete usable snapshot, ordinary shop sales, supplier deliveries with transport obligations, and ambiguous legacy records show an explanation and guidance for a compensating transaction. Review the affected goods, stock, payment and remaining obligations together when making a manual correction.

## Complete receipt and seasonal history

Trade receipts are available in pages of 50 and seasonal settlements in pages of 20, with first/previous/next/last controls at both ends. Paging changes only the visible records. All records remain in campaign backups and authorized archives. Players receive only their own trade receipts; seasonal history, internal settlement snapshots and other owners' transactions remain private to the DM.

Each seasonal record shows **Before**, **Proposed** and **Applied** values separately. Before comes from the original saved preview, including the original commodity name/unit, quotes, stock, indices and realm conditions. Proposed retains the original calculation and recorded rolls; Applied shows the approved result, including any DM override. Later configuration changes cannot rewrite these displays. If an older record lacks a readable baseline, the view says so and still displays its saved proposal and result. It does not fill the gap with today's values.
